About

Real people do not live in averages.

I am Adedoyin Taiwo, a quantitative researcher who cares deeply about health behaviour and health psychology. I am interested in the things that make it easier or harder for people to stay well: stress, routines, emotions, relationships, resources, and the changing demands of daily life.

I use dynamical systems, repeated measures, and predictive models because I want to understand people as they are: changing, adapting, and sometimes moving toward a difficult period before anyone else can see it.

Health behaviour

Why can a person know what is good for their health and still find it hard to do? I study the thoughts, pressures, environments, and experiences behind that question.

Change over time

I use intensive longitudinal data to study what changes within a person, not only how people differ from one another.

Diabetes and glycaemic health

I am working toward better ways to recognise when a person may be moving toward hypoglycaemia or hyperglycaemia.

Mental health and suicide prevention

HOW I APPROACH THE WORK

Health is not one thing happening at a time.

Stress, mood, sleep, behaviour, relationships, and biology can all influence one another. I use quantitative methods to study those connections, so that prediction does not stop at saying who is at risk, but helps us ask what may be changing and what might help.

RESEARCH MODEL

ADAPT

How does a body adapt when life becomes stressful, and when does that adaptation start to cost too much? ADAPT is a model for studying that question across biological, psychological, and social levels.

Diabetes management is never only about one behaviour. Diabeta looks at how biological, psychological, and social factors work together in a person’s everyday management of diabetes.

Teaching and data

Methods should open doors, not close them.

I teach statistics, data analysis, and research because I know how intimidating they can feel at first. My goal is not to make methods look easy; it is to make them understandable, useful, and possible to apply to real questions.

We do not just teach software. We learn how to ask better questions, work through messy data, and make conclusions we can defend.
